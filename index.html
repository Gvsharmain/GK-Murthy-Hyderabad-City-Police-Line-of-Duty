01 · BACKGROUND

The record in one view

01

Service

Official records in the case file identify Late Sri G. Krishna Murthy as a Sub-Inspector of Police. A 1985 promotion order records his posting as an S.I. in the C.C.B. The family’s later records identify service with the Special Investigation Team (SIT).

02

Death

He died on 03 February 2001 while in Government service. The family seeks examination of the exact duty circumstances and the applicable Line of Duty / Duty Nexus framework.

03

Family proceedings

Smt. G. Bhavani submitted the original compassionate appointment application on 19 March 2001. The parent department subsequently verified and forwarded the proposal to the District Collector because no suitable vacancy was available in Hyderabad City Police.

Documentary distinction: The compassionate-appointment question and the Line of Duty / Police Martyr question are separate administrative issues. The family’s LoD representations specifically request examination under the LoD / Duty Nexus framework independently of Police Martyr policy.

03 · LINE OF DUTY

A separate question from Police Martyr status

The family’s position

The 17 October 2025 representation sought re-classification from “expired while in service (natural cause)” to Death in the Line of Duty / Police Martyr status and raised a Duty Nexus argument.

After the DGP response of 15 December 2025 and the Commissioner’s endorsement of 19 January 2026, the 21 February 2026 representation expressly requested administrative review under the LoD framework and Duty Nexus test, independent of Police Martyr policy.

04 March 2026 — official forwarding

The Hyderabad City Police communication states that the 21 February 2026 application concerning LoD classification was forwarded to the Director General of Police, Telangana, for further necessary action.

DOCUMENTED DUTY RECORD

A 2001 Police order records that S.I. G. Krishna Murthy, attached to Rakshak Mobile (N/Z) under SIT, had expired on 03-02-2001 and his name was struck off the City Police rolls w.e.f. 04-02-2001.

07 · COURTROOM SIMULATION

Moot Court Rehearsal — The Telangana High Court

An educational courtroom simulation examining the chronology, documentary evidence, administrative decisions and legal arguments arising from the family's compassionate appointment case.

IMPORTANT DISCLAIMER: This is a hypothetical moot court exercise prepared for educational and rehearsal purposes. It is not an actual court transcript, judicial observation, order, judgment or finding of the Telangana High Court. All dialogue attributed to the Judge and Government Pleader is fictional. The arguments presented are illustrative and subject to verification against applicable law and official records.

Before the Hon'ble High Court of Telangana

Bench: Hon'ble Judge (simulated)

Petitioner: Son of Late Sri G. Krishna Murthy

Respondent: State Government, represented by Government Pleader

Central issue for discussion: Whether the Government can rely on a delay in administrative processing to reject a compassionate appointment claim that originated with an application submitted in 2001.

Part I — The original application and administrative delay

JUDGE (SIMULATED):
Counsel for the petitioner, explain the central grievance before this Court.

PETITIONER:
Your Honour, my father, Late Sri G. Krishna Murthy, served as a Sub-Inspector of Police in Hyderabad City Police. He died on 03 February 2001 while in Government service. My mother, Smt. G. Bhavani, submitted the original compassionate appointment application on 19 March 2001, seeking appointment as a Junior Assistant.

The purpose of compassionate appointment is to provide immediate relief to a family facing financial hardship following the death of its earning member. Our grievance is that the application initiated in 2001 remained under administrative consideration through several subsequent proceedings.

JUDGE (SIMULATED):
What happened after the original application?

PETITIONER:
The parent department forwarded the proposal to the District Collector in December 2002. On 18 March 2003, proceedings were issued appointing my mother as a Nursing Orderly. However, the family pursued consideration for a suitable alternative post, including Junior Assistant, based on the qualifications and circumstances recorded in the file.

Part II — The 2005 proceedings and age-limit issue

JUDGE (SIMULATED):
What is the significance of the proceedings dated 17 October 2005?

PETITIONER:
Your Honour, the Collectorate proceedings bearing No. A4/1303/2004 are important to our case. The record indicates that the earlier Nursing Orderly appointment was cancelled and consideration for Junior Assistant continued. We submit that this was a continuation of the original 2001 application and not a completely new claim.

GOVERNMENT PLEADER (SIMULATED):
The Government's position may rely on the applicable compassionate appointment rules, including the age conditions under G.O.Ms.No.144 of June 2004. The age of the applicant and the date on which the relevant appointment was considered must be examined under the applicable rules.

PETITIONER:
Your Honour, my mother was 41 years old when she submitted the original application in March 2001. By the time of the 2005 proceedings, she was approximately 46. Our submission is that the effect of the 2005 proceedings and the applicable age rules must be examined in the context of the original timely application and the intervening administrative process.

We also rely on subsequent file activity during 2006–2007, including requests concerning Local/Non-Local certificates, qualifications, willingness and vacancy-related verification. We submit that these records demonstrate continued administrative processing and require consideration.

Part III — The Government's position and the delay

JUDGE (SIMULATED):
Government Pleader, if the application was made in 2001, why did the administrative process continue for several years? Why was the matter not finally resolved at the appropriate stage?

GOVERNMENT PLEADER (SIMULATED):
Your Honour, compassionate appointment is an exception to the normal recruitment process. It is intended to address immediate financial hardship. A claim cannot automatically become a continuing entitlement merely because administrative correspondence continued over a period of time. The family's circumstances and the applicable rules must be considered.

PETITIONER:
Your Honour, we are not asking the Court to treat compassionate appointment as an automatic right. We are asking that the complete administrative history be examined. The family did not create the delay in processing the original application. The file itself records successive proceedings and verification steps.

Part IV — The dependent-son representation

JUDGE (SIMULATED):
What steps did the family take when the mother's request was no longer being considered?

PETITIONER:
Your Honour, on 23 August 2017, I submitted a representation seeking compassionate appointment as the dependent son. My submission was that the family situation and the history of the original application required examination, including whether substitution or consideration of the dependent son was permissible under the applicable Government orders and judicial precedents.

GOVERNMENT PLEADER (SIMULATED):
The respondent may contend that the eligibility of a dependent family member must be assessed strictly under the rules and applicable conditions. A later representation cannot, by itself, establish eligibility.

PETITIONER:
We understand that eligibility must be established under the applicable rules. However, we request that the 2017 representation be considered alongside the original 2001 application, the 2005 proceedings and the subsequent file history, rather than in isolation.

Part V — Family hardship and the purpose of relief

JUDGE (SIMULATED):
Explain the family's circumstances and why the matter continues to be pursued.

PETITIONER:
Your Honour, the family has faced prolonged financial and personal hardship following my father's death. My mother had to manage the family's responsibilities without his support. The family has also faced continuing financial difficulties, including debt and medical-related needs.

In 2025, I also faced a cybercrime related financial loss, which added to the family's financial distress. The resulting financial burden and employment difficulties have made our circumstances more challenging.

We place these circumstances before the competent authorities as part of the background to our representations. We do not suggest that hardship alone creates an automatic right to appointment. Our request is for a fair examination of the original records, subsequent administrative decisions and applicable legal provisions.

Part VI — The central question

JUDGE (SIMULATED):
The central question appears to be whether the original application, the subsequent proceedings and the later rejection have been considered together. The Court would need to examine the actual Government orders, the complete file, the applicable rules and the relevant judicial decisions before reaching any conclusion.

PETITIONER:
That is precisely our request, Your Honour. We seek an impartial examination of the complete record, including the original application dated 19 March 2001, the proceedings dated 17 October 2005, the subsequent verification records and the later representations.

GOVERNMENT PLEADER (SIMULATED):
The respondent's position is that the claim must be decided according to the applicable rules and the evidence available on record.

JUDGE (SIMULATED):
The parties' submissions and the documentary record would require careful examination. No conclusion can be drawn in this hypothetical exercise without considering the governing rules, relevant precedents and complete official records.

End of simulated proceedings. This rehearsal is intended to help organise the chronology, identify the disputed issues and practise presenting the family's documentary position clearly. It does not predict the outcome of any actual judicial or administrative proceeding.
